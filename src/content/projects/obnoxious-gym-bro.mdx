## Problem

I hate the gym and I'm bad at staying consistent. Habit apps are easy to ignore: a silent notification, a streak counter, and once the streak breaks, people quit. I want something that shows up where I already am and is a bit too annoying to ignore.

You pick a beginner program, your gym days, a nag time and an intensity: gentle, gym bro or drill sergeant.

## Design decisions so far

- **Weak and lazy, never fat.** Body shaming makes people avoid exercise, so the avatar and the roasts only ever target effort.
- **Streak repair and a gentle mode.** A broken streak is the moment most people give up, so the app is built to recover from one.
- **Telegram before web push.** iOS only allows web push after "Add to Home Screen", and a chat message with buttons is harder to ignore.
- **Curated roasts first.** A hand-written roast bank ships in v1. An LLM comes later, with guardrails.
- **Not another workout tracker.** Logging sets and reps is solved; it can integrate with open tools like wger later.
- **Mocks first.** The Next.js frontend is built against mocked APIs (MSW), then wired to a real .NET backend.

## Why I'm building it

Two goals in one project. It's a product I actually want, open source and hosted for real users. It's also how I'm leveling up on the frontend: designing screens from a blank page, typed end to end against my own API, with real-time updates and frontend security handled properly. I'm going all in on the architecture too, rather than shipping a toy.

## Where it stands

Requirements phase. The v1 feature list is drafted: OAuth sign-in, linking Telegram with a one-time token, onboarding, the daily nag, check-ins, escalation, streaks with repair, the avatar, a calendar history, pause mode, and data export and deletion. Next come system requirements, then architecture, then the build. Screens land on this page as they ship.
